# Disk failure modes at fleet scale — and how hyperscalers detect them and benchmark filesystems

Research notes compiled from Google (SRE book, FAST'07, Spanner SDC work), the classic WISC/NetApp latent-sector-error and corruption studies, Meta engineering, Netflix Tech Blog, AWS, Azure, Cloudflare, Backblaze, Dropbox, and Uber. Every claim carries its source URL. Ends with the table: **failure mode × detection method × would-a-2-hour-cert-run-catch-it**.

---

## 1. The canonical list of disk failure modes (with fleet-scale evidence)

### 1.1 Whole-drive failure — and the MTBF/AFR lie

- Datasheet MTTFs of 1,000,000–1,500,000 hours imply a nominal annual failure rate (AFR) of at most 0.88%; in the field, annual **disk replacement rates typically exceed 1%, with 2–4% common and up to 13% observed** on some systems. Failure rates grow with age ("early onset of wear-out"), and time-between-replacement is not exponential — failures **autocorrelate** and show long-range dependence (failures cluster). [Schroeder & Gibson, FAST'07 (CMU/PDL)](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast-07/disk-failures-real-world-what-does-mttf-1000000-hours-mean-you) ([PDF](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bianca/papers/fast07.pdf))
- Google's study of >100,000 consumer SATA/PATA drives found annualized replacement rates of **1.7% (first-year drives) to 8.6% (3-year-old population)**, with visible infant mortality in the first 3–12 months (drives are burned in before the data window starts). [Pinheiro, Weber & Barroso, "Failure Trends in a Large Disk Drive Population," FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf)
- Backblaze's Drive Stats (the largest public per-model AFR dataset) show model-dependent annual AFRs from **0.00% to ~9.5%** within the same fleet and year — e.g., Seagate ST12000NM0007 (12TB) at 8.72% in Q4 2024 and 9.47% in Q1 2025, while some 16–24TB models sat near 0%. Fleet-wide annual AFR was ~1.55–1.57% in 2024 and **1.36% in 2025 (lifetime ≈1.30%)** across ~344,000 drives. [Backblaze Drive Stats for 2024](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-2024) · [Drive Stats for 2025](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-2025) · [Q1 2025](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q1-2025) · [2025 press release](https://ir.backblaze.com/news/news-details/2026/Backblaze-Publishes-2025-Drive-Stats-Report-13-Years-of-Data-Show-a-Growing-Healthier-Drive-Fleet/default.aspx)
- Little difference was observed between SCSI/FC and SATA replacement rates — operating conditions may matter more than "enterprise" vs "consumer" component class. [Schroeder & Gibson, FAST'07](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast-07/disk-failures-real-world-what-does-mttf-1000000-hours-mean-you)

### 1.2 Latent sector errors (LSE)

- LSEs are sectors that have gone bad but are **undetected until the sector is accessed**; the drive's ECC flags the error only on read/write/verify. The landmark study: 1.53 million drives over 32 months — 3.45% of the population developed at least one LSE; **nearline/consumer disks were ~4–5x worse than enterprise (8.5% vs 1.9%)**; 3.15% of consumer disks developed an LSE within their first 12 months; 0.2% of drives had >1000 errors; errors are spatially clustered and correlated. [Bairavasundaram et al., "An Analysis of Latent Sector Errors in Disk Drives," SIGMETRICS'07 (WISC/NetApp)](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/adsl/Publications/latent-sigmetrics07.pdf) ([summary with the 8.5%/1.9%/3.45% figures](https://storagemojo.com/2008/02/18/latent-sector-errors-in-disk-drives))
- LSE probability **grows with disk capacity** and with age; the follow-up work (1.5M drives) analyzed how intra-disk redundancy and scrubbing cadence protect against them, and found LSEs are *not* rare in large fleets. [Schroeder et al., "Understanding latent sector errors and how to protect against them," FAST'10](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast10/tech/full_papers/schroeder.pdf)
- Recent fleet data (Facebook/Google SSD studies) reports **20–57% of SSDs experience at least one sector error** in the field — the problem did not go away with flash. [Meza et al. / Schroeder et al., as cited in "Improving Storage System Reliability with Proactive Error Prediction"](https://www.just.edu.jo/~zasharif/Web/CS775/OS-PaperPresentations/4-Improving%20Storage%20System%20Reliability%20with%20Proactive%20Error%20Prediction-act2017.pdf)

### 1.3 Silent data corruption (checksum mismatch / torn write / misdirected write)

- The first large-scale corruption study (same 1.53M-drive NetApp fleet, 41 months) found **>400,000 instances of checksum mismatches**; 3,855 disks (0.86% of nearline, 0.065% of enterprise) developed corruption. Nearline disks and their adapters corrupted an **order of magnitude more often than enterprise**. Distribution is heavy-tailed: median 3 mismatches per corrupt disk, mean ~78–104, one drive hit 33,000; the **worst 1% of corrupt disks produced more than half of all mismatches**. Corruption has strong spatial locality (consecutive blocks; average run 3.4 blocks, some runs of 1000) and temporal autocorrelation out to ~10 months. [Bairavasundaram et al., "An Analysis of Data Corruption in the Storage Stack," FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- 8% of corruptions were discovered **during RAID reconstruction** — i.e., the parity you need is already broken when a drive dies — motivating double-parity protection and more aggressive scrubbing. [FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- Corruption is not just a disk problem: one storage system had **92 disks develop checksum mismatches** (independent probability < 1e-12), implicating shared components — SATA/FC adapters and shelf controllers were observed corrupting data (models A-1, D-1, D-2). [FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- End-to-end (filesystem-level) checksumming is the countermeasure: ZFS stores a 64-bit checksum with every block pointer and repairs from mirrors on mismatch; GFS checksums every 64KB chunk block and re-reads from a replica on mismatch. [Krioukov et al., "End-to-end Data Integrity for File Systems: A ZFS Case Study," FAST'10](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/wind/Publications/zfs-corruption-fast10.pdf) · [FAST'08 related-work discussion of GFS/ZFS checksumming](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- Google has run software checksums against memory and disk errors **since the 2003 GFS paper**. [Google Cloud: Silent Data Corruption](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/10759085)
- Google's Spanner serves multiple exabytes at >1B queries/sec, and **detects/prevents hardware-induced silent data corruption events several times per week**. [Bacon, "Detection and Prevention of Silent Data Corruption in an Exabyte-scale Database System," SELSE'22](https://research.google/pubs/detection-and-prevention-of-silent-data-corruption-in-an-exabyte-scale-database-system/)

### 1.4 Lost writes & identity discrepancies — drives that lie about writes

- "Identity discrepancies" = the block read back is not the block you wrote (lost write, or write landed at the wrong disk/offset). NetApp caught these only because the filesystem embeds **block identity (inode + offset)** in each block's data-integrity segment and cross-checks it on file reads. The paper documents "actual cases when the **disk firmware replied successfully to a write that was never written to stable media**." 365 of 1.53M drives showed identity discrepancies. [FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- Google's guidance generalizes this: verify on the write path — when writing replicas, "make sure that the checksums of the replicas match," and on reads consider reading both replicas and comparing. [Google Cloud: Silent Data Corruption](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/10759085)

### 1.5 Read instability, retry storms, not-ready conditions

- Beyond hard errors, fleets see **recovered errors** and **not-ready-conditions** (drives intermittently failing to respond) as distinct error classes tracked at fleet scale; not-ready conditions affected 18% of nearline and 3% of enterprise disks over the study window and correlate with corruption. [Schroeder et al., FAST'10](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast10/tech/full_papers/schroeder.pdf) · [FAST'08 Table 2 correlations](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- Practitioner characterization of "read instability": unstable drives answer reads with intermittent errors or excessive delays, responses can be corrupt or delayed, and OSes/normal tooling are not built to tolerate it — the OS can crash or drop the drive. [DeepSpar: HDD/SSD Read Instabilities](https://www.deepspar.com/read-instabilities)
- Media/recovered errors are also *normal* drive operation (drives reallocate to spare blocks); the trick is distinguishing routine recoveries from runaway retry behavior. [NetApp KB: Understanding Media and Recovered Disk Errors](https://kb.netapp.com/on-prem/ontap/OHW/OHW-KBs/Understanding_Media_and_Recovered_Disk_Errors)

### 1.6 TLER/ERC timeout cliffs

- WD's TLER (Time-Limited Error Recovery) caps drive-side recovery at **7 seconds** so the drive answers the controller instead of dropping into a deep recovery loop — "it will only spend 7 seconds to attempt to recover... the hard drive will not be dropped from a RAID array." Desktop drives without TLER/ERC can spend far longer in recovery, which RAID controllers and ZFS interpret as a dead disk. [WD KB: TLER on RAID Edition Internal Drives](https://support-en.wd.com/app/answers/detailweb/a_id/3562/~/tler-on-raid-edition-internal-drives-cannot-be-disabled) · [discussion of the 7s mechanics](https://www.snbforums.com/threads/should-you-use-tler-drives-in-your-raid-nas.3530)
- The same class of hang shows up as latency outliers at fleet scale — one reason Meta's benchmark suites score drives on **P99.99/P99.9999 tail latency under fixed I/O rates**, not peak IOPS. [Meta FioSynth](https://engineering.fb.com/2020/11/18/open-source/fiosynth)

### 1.7 SMR deception ("SMR lies") and write cliffs

- WD shipped **drive-managed SMR (DM-SMR) in 2–6TB WD Red "NAS" drives without disclosure**; under ZFS resilvering (sustained random writes), the drive "enters a faulty state" — confirmed in iXsystems' labs (WD40EFAX, firmware 82.00A82), and the drives were disqualified from ZFS-based products. WD later admitted DMSMR's internal data-management tasks get starved by sustained random writes, recommending CMR (Red Pro/Red Plus) for ZFS. [TrueNAS notice: WD Red SMR Drive Compatibility with ZFS](https://www.truenas.com/docs/hardware/notices/wdsmr) · [WD corporate blog: On WD Red NAS Drives](https://blog.westerndigital.com/wd-red-nas-drives) · [ServeTheHome: WD Red SMR vs CMR Tested](https://www.servethehome.com/wd-red-smr-vs-cmr-tested-avoid-red-smr)
- The counterpoint: SMR is a *workload-match* problem, not an absolute evil. Dropbox runs Magic Pocket as an **immutable, content-addressed block store and was the first to deploy SMR HDDs at scale** (Ultrastar HC690), precisely because write-once/sequential workloads fit SMR's design. [WD case study: Dropbox Magic Pocket achieves exabyte scale with SMR HDDs](https://documents.westerndigital.com/content/dam/doc-library/en_us/assets/public/western-digital/collateral/case-study/case-study-dropbox-magic-pocket-achieves-exabyte-scale-with-smr-hdds.pdf)

### 1.8 Volatile DRAM write cache + power loss (drives that lie about flush)

- Hardware power-cut testing (a "Switcher" rig that kills power during workloads, then verifies data): **15 SSDs from 5 vendors, 3000+ fault-injection cycles; 13 of 15 — including "enterprise-class" drives — misbehaved**, and *every* failed device lost some data it had acknowledged; two were massively corrupted (one effectively bricked). [Zheng et al., "Understanding the Robustness of SSDs under Power Fault," FAST'13](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/fast13/fast13-final80.pdf) ([talk page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast13/technical-sessions/presentation/zheng))
- Follow-up journal version: 14 of 17 devices showed unexpected failure behavior under power faults (bit corruption, "shorn writes"). [Reliability Analysis of SSDs Under Power Fault, ACM TOCS 2016](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2992782) ([PDF](https://www.ece.iastate.edu/~mai/docs/papers/2016TOCS-SSDFault.pdf))
- The HDD version of the lie was documented in the wild in 2008: one drive model's firmware bug made it **return success on a cache-flush command without committing data to stable storage**; if power-cycled, the just-written data was lost (caught by block-identity protection + RAID). [FAST'08, §6.1.3 "Cache flush bug"](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- On the software side, "fsyncgate 2018": the kernel may discard dirty pages after a writeback error and a later `fsync()` can report success while data is gone; PostgreSQL concluded fsync errors must PANIC, and the bug class affected every buffered-write database (MySQL, MongoDB, etc.). [PostgreSQL wiki: Fsync Errors](https://wiki.postgresql.org/wiki/Fsync_Errors) · [LWN: PostgreSQL's fsync() surprise](https://lwn.net/Articles/752063) · [danluu.com/fsyncgate](https://danluu.com/fsyncgate)
- macOS `fsync()` famously does not flush the drive's write cache — durability requires `fcntl(F_FULLFSYNC)`, and even that is "best effort"; Force Unit Access is ignored by many drive types "either through lying firmware or a bridge." [CedarDB: Why Your SSD (Probably) Sucks](https://cedardb.com/blog/ssd_latency) · [mjtsai: Apple SSD Benchmarks and F_FULLSYNC](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2022/02/17/apple-ssd-benchmarks-and-f_fullsync/)

### 1.9 Firmware bugs

- Fleet data shows corruption concentrated at **specific block numbers on specific models** (disk model E-1) — evidence of hardware/firmware bugs that hit particular LBAs; recommended mitigations: staggered RAID stripes, replicate hot metadata. Adapters/shelf controllers also corrupted data (see 1.3). The cache-flush bug above (1.8) is another firmware-bug instance. [FAST'08, §6.1](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- Note: even Google found some SMART attributes meaningless because "different drive models may classify reallocations differently" — firmware behavior differs model-to-model and must be treated as model-specific. [Pinheiro et al., FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf)

### 1.10 Thermal

- The surprise result: in professionally-managed datacenters, **failures do not increase with average temperature in low/mid ranges** (lower temperatures were actually associated with slightly higher failure rates); only 3–4-year-old drives showed a modest high-temperature correlation. Utilization likewise correlated much more weakly than vendor accelerated-life models predicted. [Pinheiro et al., FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf) ([corroborated in "The Datacenter as a Computer"](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~15721-f24/papers/Data_Center_As_a_Computer.pdf))
- Thermal still matters as an *interactive* fault mode (throttling, runaway) — Meta classifies thermal runaway under load-dependent transient faults requiring induced-condition testing. [Meta: How Meta keeps its AI hardware reliable](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable)
- Cloudflare qualifies chassis airflow per hardware generation (Gen 13 E1.S drives with Gen5 controllers pulling up to 25W, validated when moving from 1U to 2U). [Cloudflare: Inside Gen 13](https://blog.cloudflare.com/gen13-config)

### 1.11 SSD wear-out / endurance (and write amplification)

- Cloudflare sets an explicit fleet endurance spec: servers live 5 years and **storage must sustain 1 DWPD (drive write per day) for the full lifespan**; their Gen 13 picks (Samsung PM9D3a, Micron 7600 Pro) meet it with ~7% hardware over-provisioning, with the option to hold back capacity for more OP; NVMe 2.0/OCP Cloud SSD 2.0 features (ZNS, Simple Copy, deeper telemetry) exist specifically to manage write amplification and observability. [Cloudflare: Inside Gen 13](https://blog.cloudflare.com/gen13-config) · [Cloudflare: Gen 12 servers](https://blog.cloudflare.com/gen12-servers)
- Cloudflare's cache fleet treats writes as the enemy: SSDs wear out solely from writes, and in 200+ cities the shipping cost of a replacement is a significant fraction of the drive's cost; moving one-hit-wonder assets to memory roughly **halved disk writes/sec** and cut disk-hit tail latency ~5%. [Cloudflare: Why We Started Putting Unpopular Assets in Memory](https://blog.cloudflare.com/why-we-started-putting-unpopular-assets-in-memory)
- The classic endurance series (the multi-year "write SSDs until they die" experiment): six consumer drives written continuously; **all exceeded their rated endurance by a substantial margin**, errors began after ~300TB (Samsung 840) with first deaths around 700TB–1PB, and the Samsung 840 Pro passed **2.4PB** before dying; performance stayed consistent until the final terabytes. [TechReport: The SSD Endurance Experiment (final chapter, via HN)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=12915515) · [ZDNet: SSD endurance — death by petabyte](https://www.zdnet.com/article/ssd-endurance-death-by-petabyte) · [LinusTechTips repost of the final article text](https://linustechtips.com/topic/327024-the-ssd-endurance-experiment-finally-they-are-all-dead)
- Endurance ratings are workload-dependent: random/small writes raise write-amplification factor (WAF), and TBW figures must be paired with the workload condition; meaningful measurement requires **steady state** (preconditioned) drives. [SNIA/endurance references via Wikipedia: Write amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Write_amplification) · [ATP: SSD Endurance & Lifespan](https://www.atpinc.com/blog/ssd-endurance-specification-lifespan)

### 1.12 Infrastructure: cables, connectors, adapters

- Google's fleet data: CRC errors are "less indicative of drive failures than that of **cables and connectors**." [Pinheiro et al., FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf)
- NetApp observed adapters and shelf controllers corrupting data, and corruption correlated across disks in the same system (see 1.3). [FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)

### 1.13 SMART-silent failures & upstream SDC (CPU/memory)

- The most sobering number in the Google study: **56% of failed drives had zero counts on all four strong SMART signals** (scan errors, reallocation, offline reallocation, probational counts); even adding every SMART parameter plus temperature, 36% of failed drives showed *no signal at all*. SMART is useful for fleet-level logistics, "unlikely to be useful for predicting individual drive failures." [Pinheiro et al., FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf)
- Silent data corruption has moved upstream of the disk: with denser silicon, Meta sees **SDC at roughly one fault per thousand devices** (vs one per million for cosmic-ray soft errors); >66% of Llama 3 training interruptions came from hardware faults (SRAM/HBM/processing grids/switch ASICs); some faults only manifest after months of "wear out" and depend on voltage/frequency/temperature — requiring random-state-space periodic testing throughout device life. [Meta: How Meta keeps its AI hardware reliable](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable) · [Meta: Silent Data Corruption (2021)](https://engineering.fb.com/2021/02/23/data-infrastructure/silent-data-corruption/) · [OCP white paper: Silent Data Corruption in AI (Google, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Intel, AMD, ARM)](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-whitepaper-final-pdf)

## 2. How fleet operators DETECT each failure mode (the playbook)

### 2.1 Checksums at every layer — end-to-end

- Google: software checksums against memory and disk errors since GFS (2003); today's guidance: end-to-end checksumming on everything that moves (e.g., gRPC adds checksums to every message), with source-side computation and destination verification; salt checksums with object IDs/timestamps to catch metadata swaps. [Google Cloud: Silent Data Corruption](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/10759085)
- NetApp: a 64-byte **data integrity segment** (checksum + filesystem block identity + a checksum of itself) stored with every 4KB block, validated on every read by the RAID layer. [FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- ZFS: 64-bit checksum in every block pointer, self-healing from mirrors on mismatch. [ZFS case study, FAST'10](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/wind/Publications/zfs-corruption-fast10.pdf)
- Dropbox: content-addressed storage — block name = hash of content (SHA-256), so corruption is detectable by construction; blocks are immutable. [Inside the Magic Pocket](https://dropbox.tech/infrastructure/inside-the-magic-pocket) · [Dropbox @ CERN: The Magic Pocket — how Dropbox is handling large datasets](https://indico.cern.ch/event/726040/sessions/296748/attachments/1787925/2915985/The_Magic_Pocket_-_how_Dropbox_is_handling_large_datasets.pdf)

### 2.2 Scrubbing (proactive full-surface reads) — and why cadence is a math problem

- NetApp data scrubs read every block, recompute checksums, verify parity, and repair; scrubs covered each RAID group **about once every two weeks** on average. Scrubbing discovered **49% of checksum mismatches on nearline disks and 73% on enterprise** — reads and RAID reconstruction found the rest. [FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- In the LSE study, **verify (scrub) operations discovered 86.6% of latent sector errors on nearline disks and 61.5% on enterprise**, vs 13.4%/19.1% by reads and 0%/19.3% by writes — if you don't scrub, you find LSEs when a rebuild needs the data. [SIGMETRICS'07](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/adsl/Publications/latent-sigmetrics07.pdf)
- Cadence math: with ~3.15% of consumer drives developing an LSE within 12 months (roughly linear in time), any two-week-between-scrub window carries meaningful per-drive risk of multiple latent errors coexisting with a disk failure. [StorageMojo's scrub-cadence analysis of the SIGMETRICS data](https://storagemojo.com/2008/02/18/latent-sector-errors-in-disk-drives)
- Scale math from the SRE book: iterating 700PB even at ideal 300MB/s takes **8 decades** — so validation must be sharded, incremental, and tiered (daily validators for the most disastrous invariants; deeper validation less often). [SRE Book ch. 26](https://sre.google/sre-book/data-integrity/)

### 2.3 Block-identity metadata (catches lost/misdirected writes that checksums miss)

- Checksums can't catch a lost write (the old data still checksums fine); embedding filesystem identity (inode+offset) in each block and cross-checking on read is what caught the "firmware replied success but never wrote" bug. NetApp replaces enterprise drives at the *first* corruption/identity discrepancy because once an enterprise drive corrupts, many more follow. [FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf)
- The equivalent application-level pattern: on write, verify replica checksums match; on read of critical data, read both replicas and compare. [Google Cloud guidance](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/10759085)

### 2.4 SMART telemetry + fleet AFR + ML prediction (necessary, not sufficient)

- Google's four strong SMART signals and their risk multipliers (first scan error → **39x** failure probability within 60 days; first reallocation → **14x**; first offline reallocation → **21x**; first probational count → **16x**) — but with 56% of failures SMART-silent, prediction needs signals beyond SMART. [Pinheiro et al., FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf)
- Backblaze's operational rules: watch SMART **5 (Reallocated Sectors), 187 (Reported Uncorrectable Errors), 188 (Command Timeout), 197 (Current Pending Sectors), 198 (Offline Uncorrectable)**; "drives with zero uncorrectable errors hardly ever fail... once SMART 187 goes above zero, we schedule the drive for replacement." They publish the entire dataset (daily SMART snapshots + failures) for download. [Backblaze: Analyzing Hard Drive SMART Stats](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/hard-drive-smart-stats) · [What S.M.A.R.T. Hard Drive Errors Actually Tell Us About Failures](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/what-smart-stats-indicate-hard-drive-failures) · [Using Machine Learning to Predict Hard Drive Failures](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/using-machine-learning-to-predict-hard-drive-failures) · [Hard Drive Reliability & Test Data](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/resources/hard-drive-test-data)
- Backblaze's failure *definition* is itself fleet software: a drive is failed when it appears in data-center work-tracking or hasn't returned to the active population by quarter's end; they combine SMART monitoring, drive monitoring tools, and automated data enrichment to separate mechanical death from temporary removal. [Backblaze Q3 2025 Drive Stats](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q3-2025) ([coverage](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/BLZE/backblaze-q3-2025-drive-stats-rethinking-failure-celebrating-high-nmcahdt2po9s.html))
- Google's System Health infrastructure collected hundreds of attributes from every server every few minutes into Bigtable for fleet mining — the pattern behind every modern fleet-telemetry effort. [Pinheiro et al., FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf)

### 2.5 Application-level validators, replica divergence, and "trust but verify"

- Google SRE's third layer of defense: **out-of-band data validation pipelines** run continuously between and within datastores. Gmail's validators detect integrity problems **within 24 hours** — teams "shudder at the thought" of running them less than daily; a significant portion of Gmail's compute footprint exists just to run validators; Google Drive's validator auto-repaired a would-be "files are disappearing" emergency into a fix-it-Monday event. Recovery mechanisms themselves must be exercised continuously ("you only know that you can recover... if you actually do so"). [SRE Book ch. 26](https://sre.google/sre-book/data-integrity/)
- Replication is explicitly *not* recoverability: replicas faithfully propagate corruption and errant deletes; diverse layers (even tape — the GTape Gmail restore and the 5,475-restore-job, 1.5PB Google Music recovery) are required. [SRE Book ch. 26](https://sre.google/sre-book/data-integrity/)
- Spanner detects/prevents SDC **several times per week** and removes offending machines automatically — detection happens above the storage layer, at data. [Bacon, SELSE'22](https://research.google/pubs/detection-and-prevention-of-silent-data-corruption-in-an-exabyte-scale-database-system/)
- Google open-sourced **CPU Check** for fleet-wide hardware-fault scanning and re-screens the fleet whenever better tests appear. [Google Cloud: Silent Data Corruption](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/10759085) · [github.com/google/cpu-check](https://github.com/google/cpu-check)

### 2.6 Performance outliers ARE a failure mode (detection via microbenchmarks)

- Meta runs **Fleetscanner** (targeted micro-benchmarks whose signatures are folded into telemetry; whole fleet covered every 45–60 days, opportunistically during firmware upgrades/repairs), **Ripple** (ms-to-second co-located tests, fleet coverage in days), and **Hardware Sentinel** (test-agnostic kernel-space analysis of application exceptions; 41% better than testing-based approaches). Performance-fault outliers have identified "hundreds of performance outliers... an emergent fault mode alongside SDCs," and ServiceLab (OSDI'24) hunts tiny regressions at scale. [Meta: How Meta keeps its AI hardware reliable](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable)

### 2.7 Power-fail validation — storm testing

- Meta's "Instantaneous PowerLoss Storm" is a standing test paradigm inside its Disaster Readiness program: deliberately validate zero-notice power loss with batteries, Power Loss Siren signals, and region-wide unavailability events — power-loss behavior is tested, not assumed. [Meta: Lights Out, Systems On — Validating Instant Power Loss Readiness](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/06/03/data-center-engineering/lights-out-systems-on-validating-instant-power-loss-readiness)
- The research-grade version: dedicated hardware to cut power mid-workload and then read back and verify (13/15 SSDs lied in some way). [FAST'13](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/fast13/fast13-final80.pdf)

### 2.8 Redundancy tuned to measured failure modes

- Azure Storage: three replicas within a stamp for hot data, erasure coding of cold sealed extents, LRC codes that cut repair I/O at 1.3–1.5x storage overhead vs 3x replication; durability tiers published as ≥11 nines (LRS), ≥12 nines (ZRS, synchronous cross-AZ writes), ≥16 nines (GRS/GZRS). [Windows Azure Storage, SOSP'11](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/csjgwang/CloudNativeDB/AzureStorageSOSP11.pdf) · [Erasure Coding in Windows Azure Storage, ATC'12](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/atc12/atc12-final181_0.pdf) · [Azure data redundancy docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/common/storage-redundancy)
- AWS EBS: every volume's data is **replicated across multiple servers in an Availability Zone**; io2 Block Express is specified at 99.999% durability / 0.001% AFR (100,000 volumes → ~1 failure/year), other volume types at 99.8–99.9% / 0.1–0.2% AFR — vs ~2% AFR for commodity disks. [AWS EBS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ebs/latest/userguide/what-is-ebs.html) · [EBS features](https://aws.amazon.com/ebs/features) · [AWS Storage Blog on volume-type choice](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/storage/how-to-choose-the-best-amazon-ebs-volume-type-for-your-self-managed-database-deployment)
- Dropbox: every block stored in at least two zones; recent blocks replicated, then buckets aggregated and **erasure coded**; cold-storage variant uses a single erasure code spanning regions; ~12k OSD machines and 650k+ disks. [Inside the Magic Pocket](https://dropbox.tech/infrastructure/inside-the-magic-pocket) · [How we optimized Magic Pocket for cold storage](https://dropbox.tech/infrastructure/how-we-optimized-magic-pocket-for-cold-storage) · [CERN talk PDF](https://indico.cern.ch/event/726040/sessions/296748/attachments/1787925/2915985/The_Magic_Pocket_-_how_Dropbox_is_handling_large_datasets.pdf)

## 3. How hyperscalers benchmark filesystems and storage

- **Meta FioSynth** (open-sourced): fio-based harness running workload suites *synthesized from production I/O traces*; suites are **rate-limited** to realistic production rates because "Facebook applications rarely push the peak IOPS or throughput boundaries of our drives, but they are highly sensitive to latency outliers" — winning drives are those with the **lowest P99/P99.99/P99.9999 outlier latency at expected I/O rates**, regardless of peak numbers. Workloads scale per-TB so different-capacity drives are directly comparable; **preconditioning** runs before measurement by default; device health logs collected before/after each workload to compute **write amplification factor**; client/server mode fans out across hosts. Used for years to catch flash-device issues before deployment and to reproduce production issues in the lab; aligned with the OCP NVMe Cloud SSD specification. [Meta: FioSynth](https://engineering.fb.com/2020/11/18/open-source/fiosynth) · [github.com/facebookincubator/FioSynth](https://github.com/facebookincubator/FioSynth)
- **Netflix** validated SSD instance types for Cassandra two ways: (1) verify the vendor's claimed *raw* performance numbers, then (2) restore **two copies of their biggest real Cassandra data store (8.5TB)** onto the candidate configurations and run the actual workload — plus careful sequencing of compactions/repairs to avoid IO overload. [Netflix: Benchmarking High Performance I/O with SSD for Cassandra on AWS](https://netflixtechblog.com/benchmarking-high-performance-i-o-with-ssd-for-cassandra-on-aws-df621335de0b)
- **Netflix NDBench**: an open-source framework purpose-built to benchmark cloud data stores under production-shaped load, with a "backfill" mode to start from hot data so you measure steady state rather than cold-cache ramp. [Netflix Data Benchmark (NDBench)](http://techblog.netflix.com/2016/09/netflix-data-benchmark-benchmarking.html)
- **Meta Arcadia**: simulate end-to-end compute/memory/storage/network performance of AI clusters before building them — benchmark-by-simulation at fleet scale. [Meta: Arcadia](https://engineering.fb.com/2023/09/07/data-infrastructure/arcadia-end-to-end-ai-system-performance-simulator)
- **AWS**: Mark Brooker (AWS database engineering) on benchmark honesty: HDDs did ~100 seeks/sec, so sub-1MB random transfers waste a spinning disk; modern SSDs are throughput-limited above ~32kB transfers and IOPS-limited below; and rules of thumb only hold "absent benchmarking on the actual hardware with the real workload" — always ask where a vendor's latency benchmark actually ran. [brooker.co.za: What Does a Database for SSDs Look Like?](https://brooker.co.za/blog/2025/12/15/database-for-ssd.html)
- **Meta's AI storage benchmark target is pMax, not averages**: hundreds of thousands of GPUs iterate over storage in lockstep, so one slow fetch stalls the whole step — they rebuilt the metadata layer (O(1) lookups), removed the dataplane proxy (fat-client SDK), added hedged reads for laggard nodes, dynamic concurrency control for checkpoint egress spikes, and a tiered L1 (host memory) / L2 (host flash) / L3 (regional flash) cache over HDD-backed Tectonic fabric. [Meta: AI Storage Blueprint at Scale](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/07/01/data-infrastructure/metas-ai-storage-blueprint-at-scale)
- **Uber** benchmarks at the data-platform level: a 350PB hybrid data lake where HiveSync (Hadoop DistCp-based) replication grew from 30k to 144k managed datasets in a quarter and now moves >1PB/day — their benchmarks and observability are about replication throughput/SLA under real dataset shapes, not synthetic disks; ML data-loading is benchmarked via Petastorm (deterministic sharded reads of Parquet on HDFS feeding GPU workers). [Uber: How Uber Scaled Data Replication](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/scaled-data-replication) · [InfoQ coverage](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/03/uber-scaled-data-replication) · [Petastorm](https://github.com/uber/petastorm) · [Uber: Accelerating Deep Learning (Petastorm optimization)](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/accelerating-deep-learning) · [Petastorm paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21275v1)

## 4. Benchmarking methodology lessons

### 4.1 dd is the wrong tool (you benchmark RAM)

- fio's author (Jens Axboe): "Fio will invalidate the cache of the file before reading it, as **without that you're just measuring how fast you can copy pages and not doing any io**." Default `dd` reads/writes go through the page cache; a benchmark that doesn't use O_DIRECT (or invalidate cache) measures memory bandwidth, and writes can sit in dirty pages indefinitely (never hitting disk) unless an fsync/flush is forced. [fio discussion #1696](https://github.com/axboe/fio/discussions/1696) · [fio docs](https://fio.readthedocs.io/en/latest/fio_doc.html)
- Concrete numbers from one comparison: buffered writes reported **3,894 MB/s** vs **214 MB/s** with direct I/O on the same setup — the buffered number was the page cache in RAM, only exposed as bogus once `fsync_on_close` forced the flush. [Surprising Results Benchmarking Direct IO vs Buffered IO using fio](https://buttondown.com/maybematthew/archive/surprising-results-benchmarking-direct-io-vs)
- fio exists precisely because benchmarking needs controllable `rw` patterns, `ioengine`, queue depth, `invalidate`, and sync options; random reads are the nastiest HDD load because seeks dominate (a 100MB/s HDD ≈ 100 seeks/s). [Linux.com: Inspecting disk IO performance with fio](https://www.linux.com/training-tutorials/inspecting-disk-io-performance-fio) · [brooker.co.za](https://brooker.co.za/blog/2025/12/15/database-for-ssd.html)

### 4.2 fio profile choices (what the fleets actually run)

- Derive profiles from **production traces**, not imagination (FioSynth); run **rate-limited** and score **tail latency** (P99.9999), not peak IOPS. [Meta FioSynth](https://engineering.fb.com/2020/11/18/open-source/fiosynth)
- **Precondition to steady state** before measuring: fresh-out-of-box SSD numbers are a fantasy of empty NAND; steady state requires cycling writes until performance settles, and WAF only becomes meaningful at steady state. [FioSynth preconditioning](https://engineering.fb.com/2020/11/18/open-source/fiosynth) · [Wikipedia: Write amplification (steady state)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Write_amplification) · [ATP: SSD Endurance](https://www.atpinc.com/blog/ssd-endurance-specification-lifespan)
- Collect **device health logs before/after** the run to compute WAF — the multiplier that converts your workload into actual NAND wear. [FioSynth](https://engineering.fb.com/2020/11/18/open-source/fiosynth)
- Scale workloads **per-TB** so different-capacity drives are comparable. [FioSynth](https://engineering.fb.com/2020/11/18/open-source/fiosynth)
- Validate vendor peak claims *and then* run the real workload (Netflix's 8.5TB restore method). [Netflix](https://netflixtechblog.com/benchmarking-high-performance-i-o-with-ssd-for-cassandra-on-aws-df621335de0b)

### 4.3 fsync semantics and write-cache cliffs

- `O_DIRECT` alone is **not** durability: on a test drive, O_DIRECT with the drive write cache *enabled* sustained 4,651 fsync/s (0.22ms — physically impossible to persist), and disabling the drive write cache collapsed it to **101 fsync/s (9.9ms)**; async writes + fdatasync sat at ~117–119/s (8.4–8.5ms). Group commit is the database answer. [Percona: Fsync Performance on Storage Devices](https://www.percona.com/blog/fsync-performance-storage-devices)
- The same cliff exists across devices: enterprise SSDs with **capacitor-backed (power-loss-protected) write caches** make sync calls nearly free (~10µs durable writes), while consumer SSDs without PLP pay 1–4ms per fsync — a 100x durability cliff that throughput benchmarks never show. [CedarDB: Why Your SSD (Probably) Sucks](https://cedardb.com/blog/ssd_latency) · [smalldatum: SSDs, power loss protection and fsync latency](http://smalldatum.blogspot.com/2026/01/ssds-power-loss-protection-and-fsync.html)
- `fsync` ≠ `fdatasync` ≠ `F_FULLFSYNC`; macOS fsync doesn't flush the drive cache at all, and FUA is ignored by many devices via lying firmware or bridges. [CedarDB](https://cedardb.com/blog/ssd_latency) · [mjtsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2022/02/17/apple-ssd-benchmarks-and-f_fullsync/)
- fsync *errors* are their own trap: retried fsync can report success after the kernel already dropped your data (fsyncgate). Any benchmark of "durable writes" must test error paths, not just latency. [PostgreSQL wiki](https://wiki.postgresql.org/wiki/Fsync_Errors) · [LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/752063) · [danluu.com/fsyncgate](https://danluu.com/fsyncgate)

### 4.4 Cloud-volume cliffs

- AWS specifies gp2/gp3 performance as "at least 90% of provisioned IOPS 99% of the time" — i.e., ~14 minutes/day of possible under-delivery is within spec, a bursting/cliff behavior invisible to short benchmarks. [AWS docs as analyzed in "The real failure rate of EBS" (HN)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43399811)

### 4.5 What a short cert run can and cannot tell you

- A 2-hour run *can*: validate spec-sheet numbers (Netflix's step 1), catch gross functional faults and DOA/infant-mortality units (Google's burn-in precedes its data window; infant mortality still bleeds into months 3–12), expose thermal/airflow failures under sustained load (Cloudflare validates thermals per generation), surface cable/CRC errors (Google: CRC errors finger cables/connectors), and measure preconditioned steady-state tail latency (FioSynth method) if you precondition first.
- A 2-hour run *cannot*: exercise a full modern surface (LSE detection needs whole-surface reads — 700PB at 300MB/s is 8 decades), observe wear-out (first SSD deaths came after ~700TB; the last survivor died at 2.4PB — 18 months of continuous writing), reveal power-fail cache lies (13/15 SSDs needed an actual power cut), trigger TLER cliffs (needs a weak sector), expose SMR cliffs (needs hours-to-days of sustained random writes plus idle-time media management starvation — that's how WD Red SMR was caught), or predict the 56% of failures that show no SMART signal at all.

## 7. Failure mode × detection method × would-a-2-hour-cert-run-catch-it?

| Failure mode | How fleet operators detect it | Would a 2-hour cert run catch it? |
|---|---|---|
| Latent sector errors (LSE) | Full-surface scrub/verify reads; drive ECC flags the sector on access (scrubs found 86.6% of nearline LSEs) — [SIGMETRICS'07](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/adsl/Publications/latent-sigmetrics07.pdf) | **No.** A 2h run touches a sliver of LBAs; a 20TB drive needs ~days just to *read* once; 3.45% of drives develop LSE over 32 months, so a healthy short run proves nothing about the rest of the surface |
| Silent corruption (checksum mismatch / torn / misdirected write) | Layered checksums (GFS 64KB CRCs, ZFS 64-bit block checksums, NetApp data-integrity segment) + periodic scrub + RAID reconstruction — [FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf), [ZFS FAST'10](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/wind/Publications/zfs-corruption-fast10.pdf) | **No.** Corruption affects 0.86% of nearline drives/41mo and clusters in consecutive blocks; only caught by checksum-verify of the *written then read-back* data — a cert run that doesn't write-then-verify-with-checksums sees nothing |
| Lost writes (firmware acks a write it never persisted) | Filesystem block-identity cross-check on read; write-path replica checksum verification — [FAST'08 §6.1.3](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf), [Google Cloud](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/10759085) | **No** (unless the run power-cycles between write and read): the write "succeeds"; only a crash-consistency test (write → power cut → read) exposes it |
| Volatile DRAM cache loss on power fail / lying FLUSH-FUA | Hardware power-cut rigs (FAST'13 Switcher: 13/15 SSDs misbehaved); fleet "PowerLoss Storm" drills — [FAST'13](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/fast13/fast13-final80.pdf), [Meta Lights Out](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/06/03/data-center-engineering/lights-out-systems-on-validating-instant-power-loss-readiness) | **No.** Normal I/O never cuts power; every failed drive in the FAST'13 study had acknowledged the writes it lost. Needs a power-fail cycle, not a longer benchmark |
| TLER/ERC timeout cliff (deep recovery hangs the bus) | Configure/check SCT ERC (7s) via SMART; observe recovery latency under injected media errors — [WD KB](https://support-en.wd.com/app/answers/detailweb/a_id/3562/~/tler-on-raid-edition-internal-drives-cannot-be-disabled) | **Mostly no.** A healthy drive never enters deep recovery during 2h; misconfiguration (ERC disabled) *is* catchable in seconds via `smartctl -l scterc`; the cliff itself needs a weak sector |
| SMR write cliff (DM-SMR sold as normal NAS drive) | Sustained-random-write + resiliver-style testing over hours–days; SKU/firmware interrogation — [TrueNAS notice](https://www.truenas.com/docs/hardware/notices/wdsmr), [WD blog](https://blog.westerndigital.com/wd-red-nas-drives), [ServeTheHome](https://www.servethehome.com/wd-red-smr-vs-cmr-tested-avoid-red-smr) | **No.** Short sequential and brief random tests look fine; the collapse appears when CMR-style zones fill and idle-time media management is starved (iXsystems caught it only in lab resilvering) |
| Whole-drive failure / infant mortality | Burn-in before deployment + fleet AFR by model (Backblaze 0–9.5% by model; Google 1.7–8.6% by age) — [FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf), [Backblaze](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-2024) | **Partially.** A 2h burn-in catches DOA/assembly defects; but infant mortality extends months and baseline AFR is 2–4% per *year*, so a 2h pass is weak evidence |
| SMART-silent failures (56% of failed drives show no signal) | End-to-end checksums, replica comparison, out-of-band validators, triage-and-remove — [FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf), [SRE Book ch.26](https://sre.google/sre-book/data-integrity/) | **No.** By definition there is no device-level signal; detection lives at the data layer, continuously, in production |
| Read instability / retry storms / not-ready conditions | Latency-outlier telemetry (P99.9999 scoring), recovered/pending-error counters, targeted microbenchmarks — [Meta AI hardware](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable), [FAST'10](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast10/tech/full_papers/schroeder.pdf) | **Rarely.** Instability is intermittent and position/load dependent; sustained error-rate monitoring over weeks catches it; a clean 2h usually sees nothing (though CRC error counters during a hard run can flag marginal cabling) |
| SSD endurance wear-out / WAF | SMART wear indicators + TBW/DWPD accounting from health logs; WAF measured around preconditioned workloads — [Cloudflare Gen 13 (1 DWPD spec)](https://blog.cloudflare.com/gen13-config), [FioSynth WAF](https://engineering.fb.com/2020/11/18/open-source/fiosynth) | **No** for wear (first failures ≈700TB, last survivor 2.4PB after 18 months of continuous writes — [TechReport](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=12915515)); **yes** for WAF: a preconditioned short run with before/after health logs gives a valid WAF estimate |
| Firmware bugs (block-specific corruption, cache-flush lies, bricking) | Fleet error-signature analytics; replace on first corruption; model-specific interpretation; phased rollouts — [FAST'08 §6.1](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf) | **No.** These bugs trigger on rare conditions, specific LBAs, aging, or power events — exactly the things a happy-path 2h run never does |
| Thermal (throttle / runaway) | Temperature telemetry + throttle detection under sustained load; enclosure airflow validation per generation — [Meta transient faults](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable), [Cloudflare Gen 13](https://blog.cloudflare.com/gen13-config) | **Partially yes** — a sustained 2h soak at full load will often expose throttling or bad-airflow enclosures; but long-term thermal effects on failure rates are weak inside normal ranges ([FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf)) |
| Cables / adapters / shelf controllers | CRC error counters (fingered as cable/connector evidence at Google); cross-disk correlation analysis; end-to-end CRCs — [FAST'07](https://research.google.com/archive/disk_failures.pdf), [FAST'08](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram.pdf) | **Partially.** Marginal cables can throw CRC errors during a hard 2h run (worth checking the counters); intermittent adapter corruption usually needs fleet correlation over time |
| Upstream SDC (CPU/RAM miscompute, ~1 fault per 1000 devices) | End-to-end application checksums; replica divergence; periodic fleet screening (Fleetscanner 45–60 day cycles, Ripple, Hardware Sentinel; Google CPU Check); deterministic-training replay — [Meta](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable), [Google Cloud](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/10759085), [OCP SDC in AI](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-whitepaper-final-pdf) | **No.** Data-dependent, state-dependent, and sometimes only manifests after months ("wear out"); needs continuous in-production screening, not a bench cert |
