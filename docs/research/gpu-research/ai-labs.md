# AI-lab GPU correctness screening: mechanisms and a two-hour bench

Research date: 2026-10-11. All claims below carry a source URL.

## 1. Decision in brief

### What a two-hour screen can establish

- [observed] AI labs describe GPU silent data corruption (SDC) as an incorrect computation without a hardware alarm. SDC can come from a silicon defect. A loss anomaly alone does not prove a GPU fault  ([Meta Engineering, 2025](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable); [OCP SDC in AI, 2025](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [inference] For a rack-bench quick pass, combine passive device/RAS evidence. Add active NVIDIA diagnostics, deterministic per-GPU arithmetic checks, and memory read-back checks. Add workload-level loss/gradient telemetry and per-GPU NVLink counters. This detects many DOA, degraded, reproducibly incorrect, or unhealthy devices. It cannot certify absence of rare SDC across inputs, time, voltage, temperature, or device lifetime ([NVIDIA DCGM diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html); [OCP](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [inference] Run a known-good workload before candidate/customer code. Isolate one suspicious GPU at a time. Preserve run/environment/UUID evidence. Classify ambiguous numeric mismatches as warning/retest, not automatic hardware failure. This matches Meta's deterministic-training and reductive-triage methods. These methods keep their reproducibility caveat  ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable)).

## 2. What AI labs report, and what attribution means

### Meta's accelerator-specific SDC account

- [observed] Meta reports that >66% of interruptions in its Llama 3 training come from hardware component failures. These components include SRAM, HBM, processing grids, and network switch hardware. This is not a GPU-only percentage. Do not present it as one  ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable); [Llama 3 report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)).
- [observed] Meta identifies limited accelerator telemetry, network complexity causing misattribution, and GPU software-stack configuration as diagnostic challenges. It distinguishes static failures (not powering/enumerating), transient load/temperature-dependent faults, and silent errors ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable)).
- [observed] Fleetscanner uses directed microbenchmarks and performance-outlier signatures during maintenance. Fleet coverage runs every 45–60 days. Ripple colocates short tests with work (milliseconds to seconds). Hardware Sentinel analyzes application exceptions in kernel space without test allocations. These are production fleet systems, not turnkey two-hour rack commands ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable); [Ripple paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08989); [Hardware Sentinel, ASPLOS 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3676641.3716258)).
- [observed] The 2025 article attributes these mechanisms to faults across AI and non-AI infrastructure. It discusses Meta Training and Inference Accelerator (MTIA) as a separate accelerator family. Do not mislabel its general fleet/CPU-capable methods as exclusively NVIDIA GPU detection. Do not confuse MTIA with NVIDIA GPUs  ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable)).
- [observed] Meta says an SDC may appear only after months. Meta cites one computational check that failed after six months on the same device. A clean short run is not a lifetime guarantee ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable); [OCP whitepaper](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).

### Training anomalies are leads, not device verdicts

- [observed] Meta describes NaN propagation and corrupted gradient variance. Corrupted gradient variance can remain numerically finite. Synchronous training shares corrupt updates and appears to progress. The drift may need weeks or months to appear  ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable); [OCP §5.1](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [observed] OCP lists data, software bugs, unstable hyperparameters, workload stochasticity, and hardware as competing explanations for loss/accuracy anomalies. Fault attribution across layers remains an open challenge ([OCP §6.1](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [inference] Stop or checkpoint the run on a loss spike. Record exact batch/seed, framework/container/driver, GPU UUID-to-rank mapping, and DCGM/XID/ECC/thermal/clock telemetry. Replay the same deterministic mini-run on the suspect device and a known-good peer. Then swap device/rank while holding software and data fixed. A failure that follows the physical GPU is stronger device evidence; one following rank, software, or data is not. This guidance derives from Meta reductive triage and deterministic training, not a published universal root-cause rule ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable); [OCP](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).

## 3. Device-level checks with credible two-hour feasibility

### First: inventory and passive RAS baseline

- [observed] NVIDIA DCGM provides passive background health monitoring for memory, PCIe, InfoROM, thermal/power, NVLink, and driver watches. Health checks report warnings/failures from kept telemetry. They do not themselves run an active diagnostic ([DCGM health monitoring](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/learn/modules/health-monitoring.html); [dcgmi health CLI](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/reference/command-line-reference/dcgmi/dcgmi-health.html)).
- [observed] DCGM health selectors include PCIe (`p`), GPU memory (`m`), and InfoROM (`i`). They also include thermal/power (`t`), NVLink (`n`), and driver (`d`). Memory incidents include DBE, page retirement, remap failure, and contained/uncontained errors. Supported health thresholds and kept history are version-dependent ([DCGM feature overview](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/feature-overview.html); [health reference](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/reference/command-line-reference/dcgmi/dcgmi-health.html)).
- [inference] The suggested operator sequence depends on the installed-version `dcgmi ... --help`. Run `nvidia-smi -q -x`, then `dcgmi discovery --list --all`, then `dcgmi health --set a` for all watch systems; use `a` alone. Wait for useful samples (about 60 seconds), then run `dcgmi health --check --group g --json`, where `--group g` means all GPUs. Save raw output and baseline counters before active testing. These flags and semantics match the current NVIDIA CLI references ([discovery](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/reference/command-line-reference/dcgmi/dcgmi-discovery.html), [health](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/reference/command-line-reference/dcgmi/dcgmi-health.html)). Use UUID/PCI bus ID as durable evidence, not ordinal GPU index alone ([DCGM feature overview](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/feature-overview.html)).
- [observed] The local rack-bench audit is explicitly passive. `rack_bench/audit/gpu.py:17-20` reads `nvidia-smi -q -x` and reports no-GPU conditions. Lines 40–90 inventory UUID/model/serial, ECC, MIG, firmware, clocks, power and instantaneous temperature. The thermal check itself says it is an instantaneous sample. It does no idle qualification or profile grading ([local source](../../../rack_bench/audit/gpu.py#L17-L20), [lines 40–90](../../../rack_bench/audit/gpu.py#L40-L90)).
- [inference] Thus audit evidence helps DOA/configuration/counter baselines but cannot show computational correctness. Record a missing/unexposed field as unverified rather than healthy ([local source](../../../rack_bench/audit/gpu.py#L24-L36)).

### Active memory and compute validation

- [observed] NVIDIA DCGM Diagnostics offers levels for readiness, failure epilogue, and deeper administrator diagnosis. It includes deployment/software checks, memory/framebuffer, compute robustness, PCIe/NVLink checks, power/thermal stress, and relative-performance checks. NVIDIA expressly says it is not comprehensive hardware diagnostics and does not replace offline field diagnostics/RMA validation ([DCGM Diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
- [observed] The DCGM memory plugin writes and reads patterns over a significant allocation (default 75% of framebuffer). The patterns include 0x00/0xAA/0x55/0xFF. The plugin checks mismatches and ECC. Its documented failures include allocation failure, read/write mismatch, CUDA DBE, and L1 tag miscompare ([DCGM Diagnostics: GPU Memory Plugin](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
- [observed] DCGM's level-3 diagnostic runs large matrix multiplies while walking framebuffer addresses and checking writes/reads. The documented default diagnostic stress period is five minutes. It monitors XIDs, temperature violations, uncorrectable memory errors and correctness. A bounded plugin example is `dcgmi diag -r diagnostic -p diagnostic.test_duration=300.0`. This bounds the diagnostic plugin only. It does not cap other plugins that `-r 3` selects, or the total level-3 suite ([DCGM Diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
- [observed] The targeted-stress plugin uses cuBLAS GEMM and multiple streams. It checks performance against a configured threshold. It watches memory errors, PCIe replays, clocks, temperature and XIDs. An example is `dcgmi diag -r targeted_stress -p targeted_stress.test_duration=120.0` ([DCGM Diagnostics: Targeted Stress](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
- [observed] DCGM level-4 memtest patterns include walking bit, address check, moving inversions, random patterns, block move and memory stress. The docs report approximate runtimes on a single A100 40GB. Tests 6 and 9 take about 155s and 244s, respectively. Default test7/test10 alternate for 10 minutes. Those timings are not a GB300 guarantee ([DCGM Diagnostics: Memtest](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
- [inference] These active checks can fit inside two hours when the operator runs them in parallel and bounds the suite. But 72 GPUs compete for rack power/cooling and may alter thermal behavior. Stage the workload. Record measured duration. Do not claim per-GPU isolation for merely parallel tests. A run that exceeds the budget must produce explicit skipped checks with reason, not an inferred pass ([DCGM Diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).

### Deterministic known-answer kernel and matmul checks

- [observed] OCP suggests end-to-end integrity checks such as checksums/hashes. It describes spatial replication/consistency comparison and integer residue checking as error-detection approaches. Checks detect but may require separate recovery ([OCP §§5.2–5.3](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [inference] Make a small known-answer CUDA kernel per GPU. Generate fixed integer inputs from a documented seed. Run integer operations with no overflow (or a defined modular ring). Copy results to host, check every element against a CPU reference, and hash the output. This is genuinely bit-exact and avoids floating-point tolerance ambiguity. It screens execution and data movement but samples only its tested code paths ([OCP §5.2](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [inference] Add GEMM sanity with fixed matrices and a reference output. Generate the reference once with a trusted higher-precision CPU or an independent implementation. Check maximum absolute/relative error and aggregate residual, not equality of floating-point bit patterns. Record dtype, dimensions, library/kernel, seed, tolerance, and reference hash. Repeating on a second GPU or alternate implementation adds useful redundancy. Shared software/library bugs can make replicas agree incorrectly  ([OCP §§5.2, 6.2.4](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [observed] Floating-point non-associativity and nondeterministic execution complicate exact comparison. OCP explains that stochastic initialization, data shuffling, asynchronous updates, distributed execution and optimization make training nondeterministic. Deterministic controls trade off with realism/performance  ([OCP §§4.1, 5.4, 6.2.4](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [inference] Never flag a GPU solely because independently executed FP16/FP8/TF32 GEMMs differ bitwise. Use deterministic algorithms where available. Use justified tolerances from healthy same-SKU/driver controls. Unexpected repeatable outliers under identical conditions merit isolation/retest. Do not loosen tolerances until errors disappear. Preserve raw diffs and compare across devices and precision modes  ([OCP](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).

### Meta CP-Bench: directly relevant public implementation

- [observed] The public [`facebookincubator/CP-Bench`](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench) repository is reachable. It contains model-level and operator-level GPU SDC checks (HEAD observed at `bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214` on 2026-10-11). Its README says it uses deterministic PyTorch runs and supports concurrent and distributed modes. It offers LLaMA, GPT, BERT, LSTM, GEMM, and ResNet model workloads ([CP-Bench README](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/README.md)).
- [observed] Operator mode executes registered operations on GPUs with deterministic inputs and compares results across devices. Registered operations include matmul, attention, normalization, convolution, and backward paths. The README documents presets as quick ~1–2 min, standard ~5 min, and thorough ~25–30 min. The thorough preset includes a systematic HBM sweep. The project itself reports the sample result (85 comparisons across 8 GPUs in 57.3 seconds). No independent source validates it ([README](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/README.md). [detector](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/pytorch_op/detector.py)).
- [observed] Current operator precision modes in code are FP32, TF32, FP16, BF16 and FP8. The generic requirements say CUDA-compatible NVIDIA GPU, PyTorch >=1.7, Python >=3.6. The project publishes no verified SKU support matrix for operator mode. Model examples/reference logs name H100 80/96GB, A100 40/80GB and GB200. This does not show that the project validates GB300 or every mode/operator ([precision code](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/pytorch_op/precision.py). [README](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/README.md). [reference logs](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/tree/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/ref_log)).
- [observed] Model mode compares periodic checksums across ranks and supports a healthy host/GPU reference log. The README warns checksum values can vary across environments and requires a reference generated for target host/GPU. Its example shows GPU 0's checksum diverging from peer ranks at step 200. Scalar checksums may conceal elementwise errors/cancellation, while peer comparison can confound device with rank/software/data path ([CP-Bench README](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/README.md). [cross-GPU code](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/pytorch_op/cross_gpu.py)).
- [inference] Prefer CP-Bench's operator quick preset over an unreferenced custom floating-point kernel for broad initial screening. Then repeat the suspect GPU alone and against a same-stack peer. Agreement is evidence, not an oracle. Common-mode driver/compiler/library bugs and shared wrong references can make devices agree ([CP-Bench detector](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/pytorch_op/detector.py). [OCP](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [inference] A model reference example sets a duration of 7,200 seconds (exactly two hours). Setup, initialization, output collection, and reporting add more time. The overall category exceeds that budget unless the operator shortens the workload duration. Operator presets plausibly fit by reported duration. The operator must still measure them on GB300. FP8 needs compatible installed Transformer Engine/kernels ([CP-Bench README](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/README.md). [precision code](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench/blob/bc4343097f9dd77b6136d4608b76617b4cf78214/pytorch_op/precision.py)).

## 4. Workload and lab practice

### Known-good first; candidate workload second

- [observed] Meta's deterministic-training strategy runs a known-effective model for a few iterations. It checks for NaNs and gradient divergence. The check only guarantees correctness for the specific values and training inputs exercised ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable). [OCP §5.1.4](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [inference] In a certification workflow, use a pinned container, model/config, input batch, seed, and expected baseline first. Capture loss, gradients, NaNs/Infs, throughput, and per-GPU telemetry. Run user-provided Hugging Face/EleutherAI code only after this baseline. That code has its own bugs, package drift, data, custom kernels, or nondeterminism. Do not conflate these software effects with hardware failure ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable). [OCP](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [observed] NVIDIA's DGX Cloud Slurm examples run Hugging Face Accelerate with DeepSpeed as workload functionality examples. They require a configured Slurm account, image, and dependencies. They demonstrate onboarding integration, not an SDC detector or universal known-answer test ([NVIDIA DGX Cloud Slurm workload examples](https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx-cloud/slurm/latest/workload-examples.html)).
- [inference] Treat Hugging Face/EleutherAI onboarding as an integration smoke after device-level known-answer tests. Verify imports, pinned versions, and model/tokenizer retrieval if needed. Run one small inference/training step. Check finite loss/outputs and logs. Passing this verifies only that workload path. Do not label it hardware correctness proof ([NVIDIA DGX Cloud Slurm examples](https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx-cloud/slurm/latest/workload-examples.html). [OCP](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).

### Slurm health policy and per-GPU NVLink

- [observed] DCGM supports scheduler integration, groups, background health monitoring, job statistics, and event/action policies. Policies can notify or take configured actions for events such as DBE. An example resets a GPU and performs short NVVS validation. Policy thresholds/actions depend on version and administrator setup ([DCGM feature overview](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/feature-overview.html)).
- [inference] In Slurm, make a conservative prolog verify GPU health before allocation. Make the epilog capture job-scoped ECC/XID/PCIe/thermal/power/clock metrics. Drain or quarantine GPUs only for configured severe evidence. Keep warning/retest paths for ambiguous performance outliers. DCGM does not enforce OS/cgroup resource isolation on its own. Make scheduler integration enforce it ([DCGM feature overview](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/feature-overview.html)).
- [observed] DCGM's NVLink health watches and counters show CRC flit/data, replay, and recovery errors. `dcgmi nvlink --errors -g <gpu>` reports per-GPU/per-link counts, and `dcgmi nvlink --link-status` shows link state. These are relevant only as per-GPU NVLink health here. This document proposes no east-west performance benchmark ([DCGM feature overview](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/feature-overview.html)).
- [inference] Capture counter deltas before/after the GPU bench rather than treating historical nonzero counts as newly caused. NVLink CRC/replay fields and semantics vary by generation. On NVLink 5, FEC history and sleep/low-power state require platform-specific interpretation. Do not fail on any counter increment alone. Compare status and deltas with generation-specific thresholds and the rack profile. An unsupported or inaccessible counter is a reasoned skip, not zero-error evidence ([DCGM feature overview](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/feature-overview.html)).

### Hugging Face / EleutherAI operator practice and determinism

- [observed] Stas Bekman's public Machine Learning Engineering book has a GPU debugging chapter with XID/ECC/row-remap/missing-GPU triage and a reproducibility chapter. It says fixed seeds alone do not ensure bitwise GPU reproducibility because cuDNN/cuBLAS may choose different algorithms ([GPU debugging](https://github.com/stas00/ml-engineering/blob/8d028c29ad1c858300a59dba2c42835f9bbc48f9/compute/accelerator/nvidia/debug.md). [reproducibility](https://github.com/stas00/ml-engineering/blob/8d028c29ad1c858300a59dba2c42835f9bbc48f9/training/reproducibility/README.md)).
- [observed] Its debug recipe sets `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG=:4096:8` before CUDA initialization. It enables `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)`, disables cuDNN benchmarking, and fixes RNG seeds. Deterministic debugging can reduce performance. Unsupported ops can raise an error. The book notes these controls are for debugging, not production throughput ([Bekman reproducibility](https://github.com/stas00/ml-engineering/blob/8d028c29ad1c858300a59dba2c42835f9bbc48f9/training/reproducibility/README.md). [PyTorch notes](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/notes/randomness.html)).
- [observed] Bekman's GPU debugging guide notes XIDs may be software/application-related and reports that some SDC may evade diagnostics. Its `dcgmi diag -r 4` mention is practitioner caution, not proof level 4 detects all SDC ([Bekman GPU debugging](https://github.com/stas00/ml-engineering/blob/8d028c29ad1c858300a59dba2c42835f9bbc48f9/compute/accelerator/nvidia/debug.md)).
- [inference] These sources help pinned environments, captured versions/seeds, deterministic reproduction and separation of software/workload errors from device evidence. They do not give a canonical HF/EleutherAI cluster acceptance suite. Run known-good operator checks before tenant code ([Bekman book](https://github.com/stas00/ml-engineering). [CP-Bench](https://github.com/facebookincubator/CP-Bench)).

## 5. Suggested two-hour `bench gpu` envelope

### Proposed sequence (not vendor-validated GB300 timing)

1. [inference] **0–10 min:** enumerate 72 GPUs by UUID/serial/PCI ID. Capture driver/CUDA/DCGM/firmware/MIG/ECC configuration, DCGM background health, and XIDs. Capture row-remap/page-retirement state and NVLink status/counters. Record unsupported fields as explicit skips ([DCGM feature overview](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/feature-overview.html). [local audit](../../../rack_bench/audit/gpu.py#L40-L90)).
2. [inference] **10–25 min:** run quick deployment and memory checks on all GPUs (or controlled batches). Take baseline and post-run ECC/XID/counter snapshots. Preserve per-device results, not only rack aggregate ([DCGM Diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
3. [inference] **25–45 min:** run deterministic integer known-answer kernels and tolerance-based GEMM checks across FP32/FP16 and supported tensor formats. Compare per-GPU throughput to a same-SKU peer/profile with clock/power/thermal context ([OCP](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf). [DCGM Diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
4. [inference] **45–75 min:** run bounded level-3 compute/memory stress and targeted stress/power. Then verify ECC, XID, remap, thermal, power-cap/throttle, and clock state. Do not use one GPU's mismatch to fail an entire tray without per-device localization ([DCGM Diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
5. [inference] **75–95 min:** check per-GPU NVLink link status/error deltas. Run a pinned known-good single-node framework workload with finite loss/gradients. Then optionally run a small onboarding workload ([DCGM feature overview](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/feature-overview.html). [Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable)).
6. [inference] **95–120 min:** reserve time for one targeted repeat of suspicious devices. Preserve raw logs and exact versions. Emit result by GPU UUID with measured/failed/warn/skipped status and skip reasons. This is a proposed budget, not a guaranteed run time. DCGM docs do not publish GB300/NVL72 end-to-end rack duration ([DCGM Diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).

### Interpret outcomes conservatively

- [inference] **Fail/hold for investigation:** hold the GPU for a reproducible deterministic mismatch, uncorrectable ECC/DBE, or failed row remap. Hold it for a fatal XID or missing/failed GPU enumeration. Hold it for NVLink link-state or counter anomalies that exceed generation-specific/profile policy thresholds. Hold it for a repeatable large performance deficit under matched clocks, power, temperature, and workload ([DCGM health monitoring](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/learn/modules/health-monitoring.html). [DCGM Diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
- [inference] **Warn/retest:** warn for an isolated correctable ECC increment. Warn for a one-off floating-point mismatch near tolerance. Warn for a temperature/power-limited run. Warn for a loss/gradient anomaly without repeatability or device-following evidence. Save telemetry and repeat with controlled inputs. Do not erase observations ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/07/22/data-infrastructure/how-meta-keeps-its-ai-hardware-reliable). [OCP](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/sdc-in-ai-ocp-whitepaper-final-pdf)).
- [inference] **Skip with reason:** skip for a missing privilege/driver/DCGM plugin. Skip for an unsupported metric or data type. Skip when an active tenant workload prevents exclusive diagnostics. Skip for an unavailable known-good reference. Skip when test duration exceeds budget. Do not represent a skipped active check as PASS ([DCGM Diagnostics](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).

## 6. GB300 applicability, confidence, and limits

### Applicability and evidence strength

- [observed] Current DCGM diagnostic documentation explicitly lists B300 among NVBandwidth-supported products. This shows only that plugin's product support. It does not prove that NVIDIA validates every diagnostic plugin, command option, or performance threshold. It also does not prove validated NVL72 rack behavior for a specific GB300 software stack ([DCGM Diagnostics, NVBandwidth plugin](https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/dcgm/latest/user-guide/dcgm-diagnostics.html)).
- [observed] DCGM documentation says factors beyond the GPU can influence its online diagnostics. It says the diagnostics are not comprehensive.
